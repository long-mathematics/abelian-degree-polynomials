## Mathematical checks

The curve note's trace coefficient is correct: the coefficient of degree `2g-1` is `-g b_u`, hence `Tr(u)=g b_u`. The discriminant estimate, power-sum pole argument, and determinant product give the asserted spectral circle. The second ending now justifies positivity using a nonzero effective pullback divisor. Rationality of the Gram matrix makes its passage from positivity on rational vectors to positive definiteness over the reals valid. Its minimal polynomial is explicitly the rational minimal polynomial of left multiplication; its squarefreeness persists over the Tate coefficient field and legitimately proves semisimplicity there. The transpose action on `H^1`, the Frobenius convention, and the distinction between arithmetic and geometric semisimplicity in the sheaf example are correct.

In the all-codimension note, the rational degree identity gives real nonnegativity without assuming RH, including denominators divisible by the characteristic. The real-root parity argument and conjugate pairing therefore give the stated paired moduli. The factor `2^g`, mixed exponents, and normalization in (7) are correct. The normalized limit in (9) is `2^{2(g-k)}`. At a strict gap the other terms are exponentially smaller even with Jordan factors, yielding a lower bound for every sufficiently large iterate. Log-concavity fills the repeated-modulus blocks, with no assumption that dynamical-degree limits already exist.

The numerical cycle space is correctly used as a **quotient** of the span of algebraic cohomology classes; equality of numerical and homological equivalence is unnecessary. The real absolute values are taken through an explicitly abstract embedding. Khovanskii–Teissier is valid in arbitrary characteristic by the indicated surface Hodge-index reduction and continuity. The non-surjective paragraph handles the eventual nilpotence above the nonzero block before applying the gap argument to the positive block. The two-map nef inequality and degree consequence follow from a scalar discriminant on every curve and do not require the maps to commute.

## Targeted primary-source checks

- **Hu, arXiv:1909.12296v3:** Theorem 1.9 gives the known abelian comparison; Remark 5.5 explicitly gives the odd-degree formula. Theorem 2.3 supplies the rational degree identity used for the parity observation. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.12296v3).
- **Xie, arXiv:2412.01216v2:** Theorem 1.2 covers surjective endomorphisms of smooth projective varieties. Section 2.5 explicitly uses Frobenius weights to tilt polygons, expose each vertex as a unique maximum, and obtain equality by concavity. The manuscript's acknowledgment of the broader theorem, Deligne input, and closely related strategy is justified. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01216v2).
- **Hu–Truong–Xie, arXiv:2503.09432v1:** Lemma 2.2 proves the weighted-max inequality `r^k chi_k <= max_j r^{2j} lambda_j`; the immediately following paragraph invokes Proposition 1.4 / concavity to finish Theorem 1.5. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.09432v1).
- **Hu–Jiang, arXiv:2609.29877v1:** Proposition 5.1 gives degree–norm comparison and `deg_k(f^n) asymp lambda_k^n n^{mu_k}` for surjective endomorphisms of normal projective varieties in arbitrary characteristic. Theorem 1.2 gives `mu_k <= 2k(g-k)` in dimension `g`, and `mu_k=0` at strict log-concavity. The note correctly presents its own Jordan-factor bounds as weaker. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29877v1).
- **Milne, Abelian Varieties, Part II, Lemma 1.3:** The proof establishes that the algebra generated by a polarized endomorphism is reduced, supporting the note's attribution of semisimplicity as classical. [Primary PDF](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/AV.pdf).
